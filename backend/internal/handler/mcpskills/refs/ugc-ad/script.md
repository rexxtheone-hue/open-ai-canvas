# Script formula (what the creator says)

## Truth first (Malaysian ad rules and platform rules)

- A generated creator is a host demonstrating the product, never a real customer. Never invent purchase, use, results, before/after, ratings, reviews, sales numbers or "I've been using this for 3 months".
- Product claims: only the exact claims the user gave you (keep them word for word). With none, talk only about what can be seen: texture, how it opens, how it is used, what is inside the box.
- Health, slimming, whitening and medical-sounding claims are allowed only if the user supplies them, and even then keep them exactly as given.
- When the person is the user themselves and they give their own first-person story, use it unchanged.

## Pick a story shape (one), then one twist

Demonstration discovery (problem appears, feature shown) · how it works · a second clever use · compare visible features with the old way · a story about something else that the product interrupts · getting ready for something that matters · day-in-the-life with a twist · staged product reveal · talking to the viewer as a character ("POV: your mother-in-law is coming") · small business process win · answering a common question · feature checklist · silent satisfying demo (sounds only).
Every shape has exactly one "but then" moment in the middle. The product enters as a supporting actor around 40–60% of the runtime; the call to action rides inside the ending, not after it. The story should still make sense if the product were removed.

## Hook (first line), one pattern

Physical action already happening · mid-sentence confession (start at word four, as if the viewer walked in) · something wrong in a normal scene, said calmly · face already in reaction, then the line · a challenge to the viewer · the product's visible action named ("This part twists open, look").

## Language

Use the language the user picks; Malaysian viewers are fine with natural code-mixing if the user wants it ("Serious, this one senang gila to use"). Keep Malaysian Mandarin and Malay wording natural, not mainland or Indonesian phrasing.

Length: about 2.5 English words, 4 Chinese characters or 2 Malay words per second of speech. A 15 s clip holds about 30–35 English words.

## Ban list

First words never: "Okay so", "Hey guys", "OMG", "So basically", "Stop scrolling", "You NEED this", "Story time", "大家好", "哈喽大家", "Hai semua". Anywhere: "literally", "obsessed", "game-changer", "holy grail", "changed my life", "elevate", "seamless", "effortless", "绝绝子", "yyds". Never more than one big reaction per clip. No dramatic pauses written into the line.

## Make the voice perform

Emotion must be in the words: stretch one peak word ("it's SO smooth"), at most one or two capitalised words per line, one broken sentence at the peak ("wait— look at this"), a quiet-to-loud swing. Calm, luxury or routine videos skip the peaks.

## Call to action ideas

"Link dekat bawah" · "点下面的链接" · "Tap the link before the promo ends" (only if there really is a promo) · "Comment 'nak' and I'll send it" (only if the brand handles comments).

## Series (only when asked for a week of posts)

Same creator and persona every post. Post 1 reveal · 2 the creator's life, product barely there · 3 getting ready with stakes (soft sell) · 4 comparison · 5 a second use · 6 how it works with the strongest call to action · 7 behind the scenes or community. Light selling first, harder in the middle, community at the end.

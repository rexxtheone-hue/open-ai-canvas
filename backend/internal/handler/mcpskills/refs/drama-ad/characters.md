# Character picture formula (face → outfit → sheet)

Every character who speaks or acts on screen gets three pictures. Only the third, the character sheet, is attached to video clips; it is what keeps the face and clothes identical across every shot. Up to 8 characters per episode; a walk-on who never speaks or acts alone gets no picture, only the same few words in every clip ("a grey-haired waiter in a white shirt").

## 1. Cast read (before any picture)

Fix a profile for each character and reuse its words everywhere: sex, age (only the script's number; otherwise the script's own words such as "an old woman"), origin (from the script or the setting; Malaysian casting by default; never guess origin from a name alone), skin tone, eye colour, hair colour/length/texture/styling, build, and one role:

| Role | Who | Face look | Clothes tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| underdog | looked down on, hides a strength | gentle, quietly determined | faded: grey, washed slate blue, washed olive |
| pure heart | strength is kindness | warm, open, serene | soft: ivory, powder blue, blush, sage |
| protector | power visible from the start | intense, self-assured stare | deep: burgundy, navy, charcoal, forest green |
| oppressor | harms in the open | cold, steely, imposing | bold: black with oxblood, emerald or old gold |
| impostor | harm behind a nice face | flawless, angelic, self-assured | pristine: white, champagne, pearl grey |
| ally | on the lead's side | natural, friendly | warm: terracotta, chambray, mustard |
| witness / functional | reacts or does a task | realistic, everyday | the colours of their job |

The hero and the villain never share a colour family. Two people who share a scene must be told apart at a glance in a medium shot: if two look alike, change one open trait (haircut, hair texture, facial hair, brows), never origin, skin or age.

Also write each speaker's voice card once and reuse it word for word in every clip: "a woman's voice of about 30, low clear alto, light Malaysian English accent, quiet and even".

## 2. Face prompt (no references, 3:4, 2k)

"Front-view face close-up of a [striking/handsome descriptor for adults only] [N]-year-old [origin in words], with [face shape], [skin tone], [eye colour], natural skin texture, and [hair, styled as the story needs]. [She/He] has [face look from the role table], wearing a simple top that covers the shoulders. Head and shoulders, whole head and hair in frame, seamless bright white studio backdrop, soft even light on both sides of the face, neutral colour, true-to-life realism, eyes to the lens."
For anyone not clearly an adult: no attractiveness words, "a natural everyday look and a calm, open expression", "a plain crew-neck top, shoulders covered". Keep a stated build (chubby, tall, glasses) in plain words.

## 3. Outfit flat-lay prompt (no references, 3:4, 2k)

"A top-down flat-lay photograph of one complete [women's/men's] outfit laid out as a single dressed figure on a plain light stone-grey linen background, vertical 3:4, like a wardrobe continuity photo. The outfit is [count] pieces: [list]. At the top, [top with cut, fabric, colour and 2–3 construction details], sleeves slightly spread. Below it, [bottom] at full length with soft folds. At the bottom, directly below the hems, one pair of [footwear], side by side, toes down. [One sentence on what the outfit is for, e.g. made for long shifts in a KL hospital.] A [cold/warm/muted/soft] palette of [colours]. Soft even daylight from above, neutral white balance, gentle shadows under each piece, true colour and texture: [materials]. Clean, crisp, high-resolution photograph."
Phones, glasses, bags held in hand, masks and weapons stay out (they are props). For a tudung or headscarf, include it as a laid-out piece.

## 4. Character sheet prompt (two references: face then outfit, 16:9, 2k)

Use a model that takes two reference images (Seedream class). Never describe facial features in words here; words bring in a different person.

"The first attached reference image is the only source for this [woman's/man's] face: same face shape, brows, eyes, nose, lips and skin tone, a full likeness match. The second attached reference image is the only source for [her/his] clothes: the same garments and footwear with their cut, materials and hardware, now worn. Create a horizontal three-panel photographic character sheet on a plain light-grey studio backdrop with thin white vertical separators, soft even front light, identical in all three panels: a wardrobe fitting of a real actor, cinematic realism, natural skin with visible pores, fine grain. [Age and build: 'a 28-year-old woman, a slim figure with natural proportions'.] [Her] hair keeps the same colour and length as the first reference, styled [styling]. [She] wears [each garment: fit, COLOUR IN CAPITALS, matte material, construction, how it is worn]. [Palette sentence.] [She] stands straight, relaxed, arms loose, hands empty. Left panel: the front of the outfit from the neck to the shoes, head above the crop. Centre panel: full-length back view, head to shoes. Right panel: one large head-and-shoulders portrait with clear space above the head, the same face as the first reference, calm, eyes to the camera. Exactly one visible face, in the right panel only. Clothes, colours and shoes identical in all three views."

Rules: natural regular fit (never baggy or oversized), matte fabrics ("matte silk crepe"), muted colours (never neon or vivid), no blood, mud, wounds or rain on the sheet (those are added per clip), no glasses or hand-held items (props).

## 5. A second outfit (costume change)

Make a new flat-lay, then a new sheet with three references in order: face, new flat-lay, first sheet ("The figure and hair styling are the same as in the third attached reference image, changed only as described.").

## Check

Look at every sheet: one face only, same person as the face picture, clothes match the flat-lay, no extra hands. Redo a picture once if it fails; tell the user in one line if a lead still has no usable sheet.

# Location and prop picture formula

## Locations (no references, 16:9, 2k, a model that writes Chinese prompts well such as Seedream)

One wide view of the empty place, used as a reference in every clip set there. Plan each location first: what it looks like, the hour (the script's, else daytime), the named light sources, and a map of its walls (left, right, far, near) with landmarks and doors.

Write the prompt as one paragraph of Chinese prose, 350–600 characters:
1. Start with "电影剧照，", the genre, then the film look: how dark the frame is, how much is in shadow, the main light and its colour. Romance: high-key and bright. Thriller: hard light, about two-fifths in shadow. Horror: dim and cold. Family or comedy: bright, soft daylight.
2. The place: type, era, size (draw a small room about twice as wide and half again as deep as real, and give width and depth in metres, so people can be staged), every map item with material, colour and position, every door (closed unless the first scene needs it open), and clear walkable floor in the middle.
3. The camera: wide establishing view at standing eye height, from where, looking which way.
4. The lights that are on, each as an object with its own small pool of light.
5. End with exactly: "画面中没有任何人，没有人影。"

Example opening: "电影剧照，温馨家庭剧，画面明亮柔和，约五分之一处于阴影中，主光是左侧落地窗洒入的上午自然光，色温偏中性。这是一间约六米宽、五米深的吉隆坡公寓客厅……"

Reverse view (for shot/reverse-shot scenes): edit the chosen location image as the single reference, in English, 60–200 words: "Edit the attached reference image. Show this same place from its opposite side: the camera now stands at the far side of what this picture shows, at the same height, facing back toward where this camera stood. Everything else stays exactly as in the attached reference image; no people anywhere, the place stands empty."
A changed state (after the party, lights off, rain on the window): the same edit, naming only what changed, with the same ending sentence.

## Props (no references, 16:9, 2k)

Give a prop its own picture only when the story turns on it, it is seen close, or it must look the same in two or more clips. A character's glasses, mask or hand-held item is a prop. Everything else is described in words inside the clip.

"A cinematic live-action photograph of [the object: outline and proportions in plain geometric words, thickness by comparison such as 'a little thicker than a coin'], [specific material and finish], resting in its natural stable position on a seamless neutral grey surface, three-quarter view, [key light direction], a soft contact shadow. [Larger than a palm: fills most of the frame | palm-sized: sits in the central third.] No people, no other objects, no brand logos."
Never use size words like compact, small or mini. If the prop carries text the clip must show (a letter, a sign, a phone screen), write the exact words into the prop prompt.

The user's own product is never regenerated: use the product photo itself as the prop reference.
